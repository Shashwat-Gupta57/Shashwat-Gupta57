Privacy · last updated 29 October 2026

Nothingleaves.

I build Talken, a messenger designed so that nobody can watch who you talk to. It would be strange for my own website to watch you. So it doesn’t.

The short version

  • No cookies. Not one.
  • No analytics, trackers, ads or fingerprinting. I don’t know you visited.
  • No third-party requests. Every script, font and image is served from this site itself.
  • No forms, no accounts, no sign-ups. There is nothing here to type your details into.
  • Two small settings stay in your browser — your theme, and that you’ve read this notice. They never leave your device.

What this site collects

Nothing. This is a static website: plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript files. There is no server-side code of mine running behind it, no database and no logging of my own. The JavaScript on these pages only animates things — the walker, the spider, the counters — and never sends anything anywhere.

Third parties

None, on purpose. Most sites quietly load fonts, analytics and scripts from other companies, and each of those requests tells that company your IP address and which page you’re on. Here even the fonts are self-hosted, so opening any page of this site makes requests to this site only.

Hosting

The site is served by GitHub Pages. Like any web host, GitHub processes the technical data needed to deliver a page — such as your IP address — and may keep it in server logs for security and reliability. I have no access to those logs. GitHub’s handling of that data is described in the GitHub Privacy Statement.

Downloads & links

Download buttons and links to GitHub, talken.in or other sites take you off this site, and from then on those sites’ own policies apply. App downloads are served by GitHub Releases.

My apps

This policy covers this website. Each of my apps that handles personal data has its own policy — Talken’s is at talken.in. The small desktop apps and games published here store their settings and scores only in their own folder on your computer; the weather widgets contact WeatherAPI for the forecast, and nothing else talks to the internet.

Why this matters to me

Talken exists because I believe a conversation should belong to the people having it — not to a server in the middle. No message server, no phone number, files in your own Drive, an account that is a key only you hold. A messenger in a league of its own.

A portfolio that tracked its visitors would contradict all of that. So this one is built the same way: it simply has nothing to collect.
